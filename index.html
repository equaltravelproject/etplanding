Planning a trip and traveling with a disability? This is for you. Run a hotel, restaurant, or attraction? This is for you too — claim your listing when we launch.

The information you need isn't out there.

Star ratings don't tell you if there's a ramp, a quiet hour, or a menu you can actually read. Existing review platforms weren't built with accessibility in mind — not in what they ask travelers, and not in how they work for someone using a screen reader. So travelers are left guessing, and businesses never hear what would actually help.

Reviews rarely mention access

A five-star rating says nothing about a step at the entrance or a restroom that won't fit a wheelchair.

The review sites themselves aren't accessible

Cluttered layouts and poor screen reader support shut out the very people who need this information most.

Businesses rarely hear it directly

Feedback gets left in a comment somewhere and never reaches the people who could actually act on it.

Access is not a luxury.

Accessibility information shouldn't be buried in comments, guessed at, or missing entirely. Equal Travel Project exists to make it central: clear, specific, and reliable — so travelers can decide for themselves, and businesses know exactly what to improve.

A conversation, not just a rating.

Every review is a two-way street between the people who travel and the places that welcome them.

Travelers share what they actually found

3

Real, specific, trustworthy information — and a direct line for businesses to actually improve.
